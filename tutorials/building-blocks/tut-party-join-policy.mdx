You build a Forge module named `open_party`. On the map you choose, any two players can form a party, whatever their empires and whatever the gap between their levels. On every other map the stock rules hold.

The rule is one party join policy: a guard that says which pairs it cares about, and a verdict that says which stock rules to drop. You check it in game with three characters, then break it twice on purpose, once with a missing `return` and once with a misspelled key, and read what the server does.

## Before you start

- The finished module of [Your first Forge module](/tutorials/first-forge-module), and the loop you use there to compile, register and restart. This module uses the same loop.
- The [party join policy reference](/docs/building-blocks/party-join-policy) open beside you, and [Policies](/foundations/policies/) for why the engine asks a policy instead of hard-coding the rule. This page uses both without repeating them.
- Three test characters on a test server, where every character can use GM commands. Two are of the same empire, call them A and B, with A at least 31 levels above B. The third, C, is of another empire. `/level <n>` sets a level, and it also clears the character's skills, so use throwaway characters.
- All three on one map, standing close together. Type `/state` and read the number after `MapIndex`. You need it in step 1.
- A text editor. The workbench on the right only shows the files; you create the real one in `share/locale/italy/system/tutorial/`.

Why three characters: the stock client hides "Invite to party" ("Invito Gruppo") on a player of another empire, and the server cannot change that. A direct invite therefore shows the level rule, and the empire rule needs the other route, a join request from C to a leader who already has a party. Do not test with the party finder: it applies only part of the verdict.

## Step 1: register the policy

A party join policy is a `(name, guard, policy)` triple. `forge.sys.game.party_join_policy.register` takes the three and returns a handle, a number greater than 0. Every time someone invites, asks to join or accepts, the engine walks the registered policies in order. The first one whose guard returns `true` gives the verdict, and the stock rules are applied with that verdict's values.

This guard matches when the leader and the guest both stand on your map. The verdict is a table of only the fields you want to change: `same_empire = false` drops the empire rule, and `level_window = 0` drops the level window. Every field you leave out keeps its stock value.

<ShowFile path="share/locale/italy/system/tutorial/open_party.forge" lines="8-16" />

`MAP_INDEX` is a placeholder. Set it to the number `/state` printed. The module returns a table, because a forge that returns nothing fails to load, and then the game core does not start.

Create `share/locale/italy/system/tutorial/open_party.forge` with exactly this content, then register it. Add one line to `share/locale/italy/quest/qc/quest_list`:

```text
../system/tutorial/open_party.forge
```

Compile it alone, from the runtime root:

```bash
./qc.sh --f ../system/tutorial/open_party.forge
```

**Check.** The compiler prints no error, and `share/locale/italy/quest/forge/open_party.game` exists. There is no `state` block, so nothing is created under `object/`. Restart the game cores and search the log:

```bash
grep open_party logs/syslog
```

You should see these two lines. The handle varies: another policy registered earlier takes a lower number.

```text
[OK] party_join_policy [open_party]: handle=<n>
[Forge-open_party]: loaded, policy <n> on map <map_index>
```

`logs/syserr` says nothing about `open_party`.

## Step 2: see the stock rules first

Leave the map. Go to any other map with A and B, close together, and have A target B and press "Invito Gruppo".

**Check.** A reads the level refusal in chat, `<Gruppo> Possono essere invitati solo giocatori il cui livello differisce al massimo di 30.`, and B sees nothing. Your guard did not match, so the stock rules held.

## Step 3: the level rule is gone

Go back to your map with A and B, stand close, and have A invite B again. Stay within a few steps: the client turns an accept into a refusal when the leader is more than 5000 units away.

**Check.** B gets a dialog that asks whether to join A's group, and it stays for 10 seconds. B accepts. Both now have a party. The log shows one create line and two join lines:

```text
PARTY: Create <leader> pid <pid>
PARTY: Join <name> pid <pid> leader <leader pid>
PARTY: Join <name> pid <pid> leader <leader pid>
```

The level gap did not matter. The policy gave this pair `level_window = 0`.

## Step 4: the empire rule is gone

A is now the leader of a party. Bring C next to A and target A. "Invito Gruppo" is not there, because C is of another empire. "Entra nel gruppo" is, because A is in a party. Press it.

The request goes through the same policy: the verdict is resolved for the leader of the party and the guest, and the empire rule is judged with it.

**Check.** A gets a dialog, `<name> desidera entrare nel gruppo`, with Accept and Deny. A accepts. A reads `<Gruppo> <name> si è unito al tuo Gruppo.` and C reads `<Gruppo> <name> è entrato nel Gruppo.` Try the same on another map first if you want to see the refusal it replaces: `<Gruppo> Non puoi creare un gruppo con giocatori di altri regni.`

## Step 5: break the policy with a missing return

A policy must return a table. This version builds the table and forgets to return it. It compiles, it loads, and it does nothing.

<UpdateFile path="share/locale/italy/system/tutorial/open_party.forge" code={open2} />

Compile, restart, make sure no one is in a party, and repeat step 3 on your map.

**Check.** The level refusal is back, as if the policy did not exist. A policy that fails does not block anyone: the engine logs the error and falls back to the stock rules. `logs/syserr` gets these two lines every time the policy is reached:

```text
Bridge::ValidateProcessorInvocation wrong return: expected [table], got nothing
[party_join_policy open_party] lua callback error
```

A policy that returns a boolean gives `got [boolean]` in the first line. A Lua runtime error gives `Bridge::ValidateProcessorInvocation lua error: <message>`. A guard fails the other way: an error or a non-boolean return counts as `false`, so the policy never applies, and the log pair carries `open_party guard`.

Put the `return` back with the next button.

## Step 6: break the policy with a misspelled key

This version spells the empire key `sameEmpire`. Nothing complains about it.

<UpdateFile path="share/locale/italy/system/tutorial/open_party.forge" code={open3} />

Compile, restart, make sure no one is in a party, and repeat steps 3 and 4.

**Check.** Step 3 still works, because `level_window` is spelled right. In step 4, C gets the empire refusal. `logs/syserr` is empty and `logs/syslog` shows nothing out of the ordinary. The engine reads only the keys it knows, and an unknown key, or a known key of the wrong type such as `same_empire = "false"`, is ignored silently and the stock value stays. The only check is to play it.

Restore the clean version with the last button: `open_party-1.forge` is also what the Solve view shows.

## Run it

The loop is the same after every change:

1. Compile: `./qc.sh --f ../system/tutorial/open_party.forge`.
2. Restart the game cores: `sh shutdown.sh`, then `sh run.sh`.
3. Read `logs/syslog` for the `party_join_policy` and `[Forge-open_party]` lines, and `logs/syserr` for the errors above.

If you pass something that is not a function to `register`, it throws `party_join_policy.register: guard is not callable` (or `policy is not callable`) into your forge.

## What stays refused

The policy lifts the two rules you named. It does not touch these, and the invite or request fails with the stock message:

- the leader is in a dungeon, or the guest is in observer mode;
- the guest is already in a party, or the party already has 8 members;
- either character is in a team session, and the two are not in the same session as allies: `<Gruppo> Non puoi invitare un nemico.`;
- the character who invites, or who asks to join, is in a PvP arena, which is refused before the policy is asked;
- the guest blocked party invites, or the leader blocked requests;
- the party switch of the server is off.

## Pitfalls

- The level refusal always says 30. Its text is fixed, so it stays 30 if you set a window of 10.
- A misspelled key is ignored without a log line. Check the spelling against the reference.
- A broken policy brings the stock rules back, and a broken guard skips your rule. Both fail safe, and both look like "my rule does nothing".
- The first matching guard wins, in registration order. Two guards that match the same pair hide the later one.
- The verdict is resolved again when an invite is accepted, so the guest must still be on your map at that moment.
- The policy judges admission only. Nothing removes a member who later walks off the map.
- The client decides what it shows. The server would admit C to a direct invite, but the stock client offers no button for it.
- Keep the guard cheap. It runs on every invite, request and accept on the core.
- A forge has no unload hook, so the policy lives until the core stops. To change `MAP_INDEX`, edit, compile and restart. `unregister` takes the handle you kept, as in [Write a battle arbiter rule](/tutorials/battle-arbiter-rule).
- The policy is per core. Both characters must be on the core that hosts your map.
