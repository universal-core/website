Foundations

How the systems Universal Core stands on work, why they are built the way they are, and what they replaced. Each foundation also has a reference page in the docs with its API and usage.

  • Luna: the framework that moves whole gameplay systems into Lua and Python, in seven pages.
  • Policies: the decisions the engine stopped hardcoding, one registry per decision, and what is still in the engine.
  • Affects: state on a character, its lifecycle, and broadcast as the channel that shows it to everyone else.
  • Traits: mechanics instead of hardcoded bonuses, with the bump point that makes a bonus depend on the moment.
  • Skills: one file per skill that the server and the client both read, the nodes a cast runs, who owns it, and how one cast reaches a map or every core, in nine pages.
  • Player metrics: one catalog of named per-character counters that C++ and Lua both feed, and the change event achievements and the codex listen to.
  • Virtual players: a temporary character on a map that never writes back, and the prize commit as its only road to the real one.
  • The headless simulator: the whole game engine driven from Lua with no client, no socket and no database, and the two kinds of harness built on it.
  • UCHTML: the client's markup engine: Jinja templates rendered into native UI, and reactive zones.
  • Render pipeline: the client's frame as a graph of passes over one device interface, typed handles, a DirectX 11 backend, and the shader cache.
